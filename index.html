Holiday scam awareness

A Pittsford guide to staying safe from holiday scams

The holiday season means online shopping, travel, year-end donations, and packages on every porch. Scammers know this, and they target communities like Pittsford with fake websites, “urgent” texts, and calls that rush you into paying or sharing information.

This page covers the five most common holiday scams and what to do right away if something feels wrong.

Read the guide

Three rules that stop most scams

  1. Slow down. Urgency is the scammer’s best tool. A real company or relative can wait ten minutes.
  2. Never pay a stranger with gift cards, wire, crypto, or payment apps. These are nearly impossible to get back.
  3. Look it up yourself. Go to the official website or phone number instead of tapping a link in a message.

On the phone with someone demanding money right now? Hang up. Do not pay. Then call your bank using the number on the back of your card.

What to do next

The five scams to watch for

1

“Too good to be true” online deals

Scammers set up fake online stores and social media ads that look very close to real brands. Around the holidays they push “limited time” deals on toys, electronics, and hard-to-find items. Often the item never arrives, or your card is used for other charges.

Red flags

  • The web address is slightly off from the real brand (extra letters or words).
  • Only one way to pay, often a payment app or wire transfer.
  • Poor spelling, broken links, or missing contact details.
  • No clear return policy.

Protect yourself

  • Search the store name with “review” or “scam” before you buy.
  • Pay with a credit card, not debit, wire, or gift cards.
  • Be wary of “today only” pressure.
2

Travel and holiday rental scams

Winter break, ski weekends, and family trips are prime time for fake rental listings. Scammers copy photos of real homes, list them on rental sites or classifieds, insist you pay quickly to “hold” the booking, and then disappear.

Red flags

  • The price is far below similar places nearby.
  • The owner refuses to talk by phone or video.
  • They want a wire, gift cards, or a “friends and family” app payment.

Protect yourself

  • Book only through trusted platforms with secure payment.
  • Read reviews, and be cautious of listings with no history.
  • Never pay outside the platform, even to “save on fees.”
3

Gift card and “family emergency” scams

Scammers pretend to be a family member, a friend, or even your boss who is in trouble and needs help fast. They ask for gift cards or money sent in a way that is hard to trace.

What they say

  • “Grandma, it’s me. I’m in trouble and you can’t tell anyone.”
  • “I’m stuck while traveling. Can you send gift cards or transfer money?”
  • “Buy gift cards for a work event and text me the numbers on the back.”

Protect yourself

  • Pause and verify. Call the person on a number you already know, or check with another family member.
  • Never read gift card numbers over the phone or send photos of the card.
  • “You must pay with gift cards” is almost always a scam.
4

Charity and year-end donation scams

People are generous at the end of the year. Scammers invent charities, or copy the name of a real one with a small change, to trick donors.

Red flags

  • High-pressure requests to give right now by phone or text.
  • No clear answer on how the money will be used.
  • Donations accepted only by wire, gift cards, or payment apps.

Protect yourself

  • Search the charity’s name with “review” or “rating” before giving.
  • Go to the charity’s official website yourself instead of following a link.
  • Keep records of what you give.
5

Package and delivery text scams

Scammers send texts and emails that look like they come from UPS, FedEx, USPS, or Amazon, with a link to “track your package” or pay a small fee.

Red flags

  • The message comes from a random phone number or email address.
  • The link looks strange, with extra words or numbers.
  • It says your package will be returned or destroyed unless you act now.

Protect yourself

  • Do not tap links in texts or emails you did not expect.
  • Open the shipper’s official site or app and type in your tracking number.
  • Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM), then delete them.
!

If you already paid or shared information

Act quickly, and do not be embarrassed: these scams are designed by professionals and fool careful people every day. Even if you cannot get all your money back, moving fast limits the damage and helps investigators.

If you gave a credit or debit card number

  • Call the number on the back of your card right away and explain what happened.
  • Ask the bank to review recent charges and block anything suspicious.
  • Change your online banking password and turn on account alerts.

If you paid with gift cards

  • Contact the company that issued the card right away and say it was used in a scam.
  • Keep the receipt and the card itself.
  • Report it to the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

If personal information was exposed

  • Change passwords on every account that shares that login or email.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever you can.
  • Check your credit reports for free at AnnualCreditReport.com and look for accounts you do not recognize.
  • Follow the step-by-step recovery plan at IdentityTheft.gov.
Not sure what to do next? Talk with your bank, a trusted family member, or local law enforcement. Asking early is always better than waiting.

Who to call

  • Your bank or credit union Use the number on the back of your card and ask for the fraud department.
  • Local police or sheriff Use the non-emergency line to file a report. Call 911 only if you are in danger.
  • ReportFraud.ftc.gov Federal Trade Commission. Report any scam.
  • ic3.gov FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center, for online fraud.
  • New York Attorney General Consumer helpline: 1-800-771-7755
  • AARP Fraud Watch: 877-908-3360 Free helpline for anyone, staffed by trained volunteers.

Pass it on

The best defense is a community that talks to each other. Share this guide, bring it up at neighborhood gatherings, and encourage older relatives to call someone they trust before sending money.

Why this page exists

Holiday scams affect people in every community. This guide was created to help Pittsford and Monroe County residents recognize common tactics and take simple, practical steps to stay safe.
