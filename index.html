Holiday scam awareness

How to spot a holiday scam, and what to do next

Scammers are busiest in November and December. They use fake websites, urgent texts, and phone calls to rush you into paying or sharing personal information. This short guide shows you what to look for.

Read the guide

Three rules that stop most scams

  1. Slow down. Scammers want you to rush. A real company or relative can wait ten minutes.
  2. Never pay a stranger with gift cards, wire transfers, crypto, or apps like Zelle and Venmo. That money is almost impossible to get back.
  3. Look it up yourself. Do not tap the link or call the number in a message. Find the real website or phone number on your own.

On the phone with someone demanding money right now? Hang up. Do not pay. Then call your bank using the number on the back of your card.

What to do next
True all year: your bank, Medicare, Social Security, the IRS, and the police will never call to tell you to move your money, buy gift cards, or let them into your computer. If a caller asks for any of these, hang up.

Five holiday scams to watch for

1

Fake online stores

Fake stores and social media ads copy real brands and offer deals that seem too good to be true. You pay, and nothing arrives.

Red flags

  • The web address is a little different from the real store’s.
  • The price is far lower than anywhere else.
  • You can only pay by payment app, wire transfer, or gift card.

Protect yourself

  • Search for the store’s name plus the word “scam” before you buy.
  • Pay with a credit card. It is the easiest to dispute.
  • Ignore “today only” pressure.
2

Fake vacation rentals

Scammers copy photos of real homes and post them as rentals. They ask for a quick payment to “hold” your booking, then disappear.

Red flags

  • The price is far below similar places nearby.
  • The owner will not talk by phone or video.
  • They ask you to pay outside the booking website.

Protect yourself

  • Book through a well-known rental website.
  • Read the reviews. Be careful with brand-new listings.
  • Never pay outside the website, even to “save on fees.”
3

“Family emergency” calls and gift cards

A caller pretends to be a grandchild, a friend, or your boss who is in trouble and needs money fast. Scammers can now use software to copy a real person’s voice, so the caller may sound exactly like someone you love.

What they say

  • “Grandma, it’s me. I’m in trouble. Please don’t tell anyone.”
  • “I’m stuck and need money right now.”
  • “Buy gift cards and read me the numbers on the back.”

Protect yourself

  • Hang up. Call the person back on the number you already have.
  • Agree on a family code word, and ask for it.
  • Never read gift card numbers to anyone over the phone.
4

Fake charities

People give generously in December. Scammers invent charities, or use a name that sounds almost like a real one.

Red flags

  • They push you to give right now.
  • They cannot explain how the money is used.
  • They only take wire transfers, gift cards, or payment apps.

Protect yourself

  • Look the charity up before you give.
  • Donate on the charity’s own website, not through a link.
  • Keep a record of what you gave.
5

Fake package texts

A text or email says there is a problem with your delivery and asks you to tap a link or pay a small fee. It looks like it came from USPS, UPS, FedEx, or Amazon. It did not.

Red flags

  • You were not expecting the message.
  • The link looks odd, with extra words or numbers.
  • It says your package will be returned unless you act now.

Protect yourself

  • Do not tap the link.
  • Go to the shipper’s real website and type in your tracking number.
  • Forward the text to 7726 (it spells SPAM), then delete it.
!

If you already paid or shared information

Act fast, and do not be embarrassed. These scams are run by professionals and fool careful people every day.

If you gave a card or bank account number

  • Call the number on the back of your card now. Tell them what happened.
  • Ask them to block the charges and send you a new card.
  • Change your online banking password.

If you paid with gift cards

  • Call the gift card company now, using the number on the card. They may be able to freeze the money.
  • Keep the card and the receipt.
  • Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

If you shared a password or Social Security number

  • Change your passwords, starting with email and banking.
  • Turn on two-step login, where a code is sent to your phone.
  • Follow the step-by-step plan at IdentityTheft.gov.
  • Check your credit reports for free at AnnualCreditReport.com.
Not sure what to do? Call one of the free helplines under Who to call, or talk with a family member you trust. Asking early is always better than waiting.

Who to call

  • Your bank or credit union Use the number on the back of your card. Ask for the fraud department.
  • Lifespan of Greater Rochester 585-244-8400 Local help for older adults who suspect a scam.
  • AARP Fraud Watch Helpline 877-908-3360 Free. Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.
  • New York Attorney General 1-800-771-7755 Consumer helpline, or visit ag.ny.gov.
  • Monroe County Sheriff File a report if you lost money. Call 911 if you are in danger.
  • Report it online Any scam: ReportFraud.ftc.gov
